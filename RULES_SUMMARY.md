# DND2: Complete Rules and Decisions Summary

Updated: 16 October 2026 · Master reference for the DND2 tabletop RPG and its future website

This is the updated summary of everything we've discussed and decided for DND2 so far, including the newer decisions about stats, movement, turn order, Turn Time, exploration, and how the website fits into your tabletop sessions.

I've separated confirmed decisions from unfinished rules so we don't accidentally treat an idea or example as a final rule.

---

## 1. The vision for DND2

DND2 is a multiverse tabletop RPG that combines strategic, turn-based combat with a chaotic power fantasy.

Players can use existing fictional characters or create original characters. Characters from completely different universes can adventure together, become stronger, find equipment, learn abilities, fight powerful enemies, and save worlds.

For example, a party could contain Goku, SpongeBob, Obama, and an original character. The goal is to let unusual combinations work within a consistent game system without requiring every character to have the same personality, abilities, or playstyle.

### How the game will actually be played

DND2 is still a tabletop roleplaying game, not simply a video game played through a browser.

- **Roleplaying and exploration**: Players sit on a voice call, imagine travelling through forests and towns, interact with NPCs, investigate locations, solve puzzles, and make decisions.
- **Combat**: When a structured battle begins, the group uses the DND2 website to handle turns, movement, attacks, defence, abilities, and other mechanics.
- **Dice outside combat**: The same game system applies to meaningful uncertain actions, such as opening a suspicious chest, finding hidden objects, persuading someone, sneaking past guards, disarming traps, or lifting a heavy gate.
- **The DM**: The DM describes the world, controls NPCs and enemies, determines when checks are necessary, and narrates the consequences of player actions.
- **The website**: The website supports the tabletop experience. It handles mechanics and tracking rather than replacing conversation, imagination, or roleplaying.

Not every action requires a roll. A roll is generally useful when an outcome is uncertain and the result matters.

Characters retain their stats, abilities, equipment, and progression whether they're exploring or fighting.

## 2. Character creation and balancing

### Existing and original characters

Characters can come from existing fictional universes or be completely original creations. Both use the same underlying DND2 rules.

A character's identity is represented through:
- Their stats and how the player allocates progression points.
- Their signature moves and abilities.
- Their innate passive abilities.
- Their equipment.
- Their weaknesses, resistances, and special mechanics.

### Balancing characters

Characters of the same level begin with the same base stats, regardless of their original universe or canonical power.

A character does not automatically receive higher stats just because they are exceptionally powerful in their original story.

Instead, character differences come from abilities, innate passives, equipment, and the player's stat allocation. Characters at the same level should be approximately equal in overall power, even if they achieve that power in very different ways.

This is a balancing target rather than a guarantee that every possible custom ability will be equally strong.

### Starting characters

**Confirmed:**
- Characters begin at Level 1.
- Every character receives five starting skill points.
- All characters use the same base-stat framework.
- Characters can spend starting skill points to customise their stats.
- Characters can gain additional skill points when levelling up, although the amount per level has not yet been decided.

## 3. Character stats

DND2 currently has **ten stats** (after splitting Defence into Physical and Magic Defence).

| Stat | Starting value | Maximum | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **HP** | 20 HP | No fixed maximum decided | Determines health and how much damage a character can survive. |
| **ATK** | 1 | No fixed maximum decided | Used for physical attack damage and strength-like checks (e.g. lifting gates). |
| **P.DEF** | 1 | No fixed maximum decided | Flat physical damage reduction when not actively blocking. |
| **M.DEF** | 1 | No fixed maximum decided | Flat magic damage reduction when not actively blocking. |
| **Move** | 5 | 10 | Movement range on the battle grid (orthogonal diamond). |
| **Jump** | 0 | 5 | Obstacle and unit jumping capabilities. |
| **Intelligence** | 1 | No fixed maximum decided | Magic-type attack power and intelligence-based checks/puzzles. |
| **Luck** | 1 | No fixed maximum decided | Luck checks, discovery of hidden items, critical outcomes. |
| **Accuracy** | 1 | 5 | Adds directly to d20 attack rolls to shift outcome brackets. |
| **Speed** | 1 | No fixed maximum decided | Determines round turn order (highest acts first). |

The starting-value rule is that every stat starts at 1 except:
- HP starts at 20.
- Move starts at 5.
- Jump starts at 0.

### HP increases

**Confirmed:**
- The starting HP value is 20.
- Each increase to the HP stat adds 5 maximum HP through the shared skill-point system.
- **Progression model (Question 118 Confirmed - Option C: Both):** Characters receive an automatic HP increase on every level-up, AND can also choose to spend skill points into HP (+5 maximum HP per point) to specialize.
- **Automatic HP per level (Question 119 Confirmed):** Characters gain **+5 maximum HP automatically** every level-up.

### Stat limits

The current rules are:
- Move has a maximum of 10.
- Jump has a maximum of 5.
- Accuracy has a maximum of 5.
- HP, ATK, P.DEF, M.DEF, Intelligence, Luck, and Speed have no fixed maximum established.

### How stats affect actions

The intended uses are:
- **ATK:** Physical attack damage ($\text{Damage} = \text{Base} + \text{ATK}$) and physical-strength tasks.
- **P.DEF:** Passive flat physical damage reduction ($\text{Incoming Physical Damage} - \text{P.DEF}$, minimum 1).
- **M.DEF:** Passive flat magic damage reduction ($\text{Incoming Magic Damage} - \text{M.DEF}$, minimum 1).
- **Intelligence:** Magic-type attack damage and intelligence-based checks.
- **Luck:** Luck-based checks, critical bonuses, and discoveries.
- **Accuracy:** Attack-roll modifier ($\text{d20} + \text{Accuracy}$).
- **Speed:** Turn order recalculation each round.
- **Move:** Movement range.
- **Jump:** Obstacle traversal and jumping over units.
- **HP:** Maximum health pool.

## 4. Skill points and levelling

### Confirmed skill-point rules

- Characters receive five starting skill points at Level 1.
- All stats use one shared pool of skill points.
- Players choose which stats to improve.
- Every stat increase normally costs one skill point.
- Increasing Move by one costs one skill point (capped at 10).
- Increasing Jump by one costs one skill point (capped at 5).
- Accuracy costs one skill point per increase (capped at 5).
- Increasing HP by one adds five maximum HP.
- **Skill points per level-up (Confirmed - Question 120):** Characters receive **2 fixed skill points** at every level-up.
- **Automatic HP growth (Confirmed - Question 119):** Characters automatically gain **+5 maximum HP** upon every level-up (equivalent to 1 free HP stat point).
- **Level cap (Confirmed - Question 121):** The maximum level cap is **Level 50**.
  - A Level 50 character with no points spent in HP has a baseline of **265 HP** ($20 + 49 \times 5$).
  - Over 50 levels, a character earns **103 total skill points** (5 starting + $49 \times 2$), allowing deep specialization while respecting the hard caps on Move, Jump, and Accuracy (which together require only 14 points to max out from base).
  - A custom progression system will apply to characters who reach Level 50.

### Level progression

**Confirmed:**
- Characters gain experience from battles, quests, exploration, story achievements, and other meaningful accomplishments.
- Experience rewards should reflect the challenge or significance of the activity.
- Characters gain levels and receive skill points when levelling up.
- The level cap is fixed at **Level 50**.
- There will be a custom progression system for characters who reach Level 50.

**Still undecided:**
- Experience required for each level (XP curve).
- How excess experience works after Level 50 (e.g. prestige perks, mastery tokens).
- Whether stat respecs cost currency, a special item, or something else.

### Respecs (Confirmed - Question 138)

Players can completely reset and reallocate their spent skill points using a rare consumable token: the **Multiverse Prism**.
- Consuming a Multiverse Prism refunds all allocated skill points back to the player's pool, allowing full redistribution.
- Multiverse Prisms are rare, valuable treasures earned through major quest completions, defeating formidable optional bosses, or purchased at high prices from exotic multiverse merchants.
- Respecs can only be performed outside of combat during periods of downtime or rest.

## 5. Dice and checks

DND2 uses a custom dice system.

### Main die

A d20 is the default die for most checks, although other dice may be used when appropriate for a particular mechanic. Stats, equipment, abilities, and circumstances can modify the result.

*Dice determine the outcome; relevant stats and modifiers influence the result.*

### Check Formula & Stat Modifiers (Confirmed - Question 128A)
For any uncertain action outside or inside exploration, the player rolls:
$$\text{Check Total} = \text{d20} + \text{Relevant Stat} + \text{Situational Modifiers}$$
- Lifting a massive gate: $\text{d20} + \text{ATK}$
- Deciphering an ancient alien rune or solving a puzzle: $\text{d20} + \text{Intelligence}$
- Discovering a concealed compartment / spotting hidden loot: $\text{d20} + \text{Luck}$
- Persuading, threatening, or outwitting an NPC: DM assigns relevant stat based on character's approach (e.g. ATK for brute intimidation, INT for reasoned debate, Luck for a wild gamble).

### Standard Difficulty Class (DC) Benchmarks (Confirmed - Question 128B)
The DM assigns a target DC based on the multiverse ladder:

| DC | Difficulty Rating | Example Action |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **5** | Trivial | Spotting a campfire at night, kicking open a broken wooden door. |
| **10** | Easy | Climbing a ladder with missing rungs, recalling local lore. |
| **15** | Moderate | Picking an average lock, hearing quiet footsteps behind a wall. |
| **20** | Hard | Deciphering forgotten planar dialects, disarming a master arcane trap. |
| **25** | Very Hard | Lifting a steel portcullis single-handedly, out-negotiating a planar demon. |
| **30+** | Multiverse Legendary | Performing reality-defying stunts, solving celestial riddles. |

### Natural 1 and Natural 20 on Checks (Confirmed - Question 128C)
- **Natural 20 (Narrative Triumph):** Represents the best possible plausible outcome given the character's approach. Yields bonus discovery, extra speed, stylistic flair, or an unexpected secondary boon.
- **Natural 1 (Narrative Complication):** Does not break physical reality or cause instant death, but introduces a major narrative complication, hilarious setback, loss of time, or heightened enemy alertness.

## 6. Combat structure

Combat is turn-based and uses a grid when tactical positioning matters.

### Main action

Every character normally receives one main action per turn:
- Using a basic attack.
- Using an equipped move.
- Entering a defensive stance.

Movement is separate from the main action. A character can move and then take an action, or stay stationary and act/defend. Defensive stance consumes the main action.

### Turn sequence

1. The character moves, if desired.
2. The character takes their main action.
3. Their turn ends.

Movement cannot normally be split around the main action without a special ability.

### Turn order & Speed tie-breakers (Confirmed - Question 126A)

Turn order is based on Speed: fastest acts first, slowest acts later.
- **Speed Tie-Breakers:** When combatants have identical Speed values, the **Party always has priority over enemies** (Player Advantage).
  - Among players/allies with identical Speed, the players decide their acting order freely (or roll an opposed d20 if desired).
  - Among multiple enemies with identical Speed, the DM decides or follows a set initiative queue.

### Speed recalculation & Buff timing (Confirmed - Question 126B)

- At the start of each new round, current Speed values are evaluated and turn order is recalculated.
- **Mid-Round Buff/Debuff Timing:** Any Speed changes applied during a round (e.g., haste, slows, speed debuffs) **take effect at the start of the next round**. This preserves round stability, preventing combatants from being skipped or taking double turns abruptly mid-round.

## 7. Turn Time (TT) and extra turns (Confirmed - Question 127)

TT operates via an **Accumulating Action Gauge** system (0% to 100%):
- **Turn Threshold:** When a combatant's Action Gauge reaches **100%**, they take a turn. Once the turn completes, their gauge resets to 0% (any overflow carries over into the next cycle).
- **Gauge Fill Rate (per tick):**
  $$\text{Gauge Gain per Tick} = \text{Speed} \times \text{TT Multiplier}$$
  - The baseline TT Multiplier is **$1.0\times$ (100% TT)**.
  - An item or buff granting **+50% TT** increases the multiplier to **$1.5\times$**, causing the character's gauge to accumulate 50% faster, yielding 3 turns for every 2 turns a baseline unit of equal Speed receives.
  - A debuff of **-50% TT** reduces the multiplier to **$0.5\times$**, halving turn frequency.
- **Simultaneous 100% Gauge Ties:** If multiple combatants hit 100% on the same tick, tie-breakers follow:
  1. Party Priority (Player Advantage: allies act before enemies).
  2. Higher base Speed.
  3. Player choice (or opposed d20 roll).

## 8. Attack rolls and damage

### Basic attacks
- Universal to all characters; costs no special resource; consumes main action.
- Deals damage only (no inherent secondary effects).
- Can critically hit.
- **Damage Formula (Confirmed - Question 122):**
  $$\text{Damage} = \text{Base Damage} + \text{ATK}$$
  - Standard base damage is **5** (unarmed / baseline character attack), which can be modified by equipped weapons or character signature styles (e.g. Goku's Martial Strike = Base 6 + ATK).
  - *Example:* At Level 1 with 1 ATK, standard basic attack deals $5 + 1 = 6$ damage before DEF reduction.
- **Attack Range & Range Bands (Confirmed):**
  Every character and attack defines an **Attack Range**:
  - **Melee (Range 1):** The attacker must be directly adjacent (1 orthogonal or diagonal grid step) to the target.
  - **Single Range Limit (e.g. Range 1–4):** The target can be anywhere from adjacent up to 4 tiles away.
  - **Range Bands / Dead Zones (e.g. Range 5–9):** The target must be within the specified minimum and maximum tile distance ($[R_{\min}, R_{\max}]$). Characters with minimum ranges (like archers, snipers, or siege blasters) cannot target units closer than their minimum range without repositioning or using a secondary melee move.
  - Distance is measured using standard grid distance (Manhattan or Chebyshev distance based on grid mode).

### Attack-roll outcomes & Accuracy mechanics (Confirmed - Question 123)
Combat uses a d20 roll modified by Accuracy: $\text{Total Roll} = \text{d20} + \text{Accuracy}$.

The natural boundaries (1 and 20) are absolute, while Accuracy shifts the intermediate rolls across the tactical brackets:

| Roll / Outcome | Condition | Result & Tactical Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Natural 1** | Die shows 1 (unmodified) | **Critical Miss:** Deals 0 damage, turn action wasted. DM applies an additional situational disadvantage. |
| **Enemy-Favoured** | Modified total $\le 9$ | **Flawed Hit:** Attack hits for normal damage, but activates an **Enemy Tactical Perk**. |
| **Average Hit** | Modified total $= 10$ | **Standard Hit:** Clean hit dealing standard calculated damage with no additional tactical side-effects. |
| **Party-Favoured** | Modified total $\ge 11$ (and not Nat 20) | **Advantaged Hit:** Attack hits for normal damage and activates a **Party Tactical Perk**. |
| **Natural 20** | Die shows 20 (unmodified) | **Critical Hit:** Calculated damage is doubled ($2 \times \text{Damage}$), plus DM/system awards a major party tactical consequence. |

### Tactical Perks Menu (Confirmed - Question 124)
When a hit triggers a tactical outcome, the controlling side selects 1 tactical perk from the standard menu (lasts until the affected unit's next turn) or uses DM Custom:

**Party-Favoured Perks (Attacker chooses 1):**
1. **Shove:** Push target 1 grid tile away.
2. **Expose:** Target suffers **-2 P.DEF or -2 M.DEF** against the next incoming attack.
3. **Assist:** The next ally attacking this target gains **+2 to their attack roll**.
4. **DM Custom:** The DM adjudicates a unique narrative/environmental tactical benefit.

**Enemy-Favoured Perks (Target/DM chooses 1):**
1. **Reposition:** Target shifts 1 grid tile freely away or to cover.
2. **Brace:** Target gains **+2 P.DEF or +2 M.DEF** against the next incoming attack.
3. **Opening:** Target gains **+2 to their next attack roll** against the attacker.
4. **DM Custom:** The DM adjudicates a unique narrative/environmental complication.

## 9. Defence, blocking, and parrying

### Passive Defence & Damage Reduction (Confirmed - Questions 125A & 125B)
When a character is not actively blocking, incoming damage is reduced flatly by the appropriate defensive stat:
- **Physical Attacks:**
  $$\text{Damage received} = \max(1, \text{Incoming Physical Damage} - \text{P.DEF})$$
- **Magic Attacks:**
  $$\text{Damage received} = \max(1, \text{Incoming Magic Damage} - \text{M.DEF})$$

**Minimum Damage Rule:** A successful hit that connects always deals a minimum of **1 chip damage**, preventing targets from becoming completely invulnerable. Only active blocks rolling 100% (Natural 20) or specific invulnerability moves can reduce damage to 0.

### Defensive stances & Active block rolls (Confirmed - Question 132)
- Consumes main action, lasts until start of character's next turn.
- Defender rolls separate d20 against each incoming attack to determine percentage blocked:
  - 1: 0%
  - 2: 5% ... (+5% per pip) ...
  - 19: 90%
  - 20: 100% block + Parry.
- **Parry**: Deals damage = $\text{round\_half\_up}(\text{Defender ATK} / 10)$.
- Passive P.DEF / M.DEF does not stack on top of active block percentage.
- Damage rounded: $<0.5$ down, $\ge 0.5$ up.
- **Area-of-Effect (AoE) Attacks:** Active defensive stance can brace against AoE blasts, but the rolled block percentage is **halved** (e.g. rolling 14 blocks $65\% / 2 = 32.5\% \rightarrow 33\%$).
- **Ambushes & Blind Flanks:** Attacks delivered from total surprise, stealth, or direct blind angles bypass active block stance entirely; the defender must rely solely on passive P.DEF / M.DEF.

## 10. Movement, Terrain, and Obstacles (Confirmed - Questions 129–131)

### Basic Movement
- Move starts at 5 (maximum 10).
- Jump starts at 0 (maximum 5).
- Movement range is measured in an orthogonal diamond grid.
- A character completes their movement before taking their main action (cannot split movement around an action unless a special perk explicitly allows it).

### Terrain Movement Costs (Question 130)
- **Normal Terrain:** 1 movement space per grid tile.
- **Difficult Terrain (Mud, shallow water, heavy rubble, steep stairs):** Costs **2 movement spaces** per tile entered.
- **Hazardous Terrain (Lava, spikes, acid, burning floor):** Normal movement cost, but character takes flat hazard damage immediately upon entering the tile (e.g., 5 flat damage).
- **Impassable Obstacles (Solid stone pillars, bottomless abysses):** Cannot be entered horizontally.

### Official Battlefield Layout Types & Special Mechanics (Confirmed)
The DND2 system and website feature **5 core battlefield layout presets**, each with its own top-down illustrated background and tactical mechanics:

1. **Plains (Open Field — $10\times10$):**
   - **Layout:** Wide-open grassy field with **zero obstacles** and **zero elevation changes** ($H0$ everywhere).
   - **Tactical Focus:** Pure spacing, movement speed, and ranged dead-zone management without cover or chokepoints.
2. **Mountainous Region (Rocks & Scalable Spires — $10\times10$):**
   - **Layout:** Craggy rock formations and **scalable stone spires** ($H1, H2, H3$).
   - **High Ground Range Advantage:** Standing atop an elevated spire grants ranged attackers **+1 Maximum Attack Range per height level** ($+H$ max range), rewarding characters who invest in `Jump` to scale high perches.
3. **Dungeon Room (Standard — $10\times10$):**
   - **Layout:** Enclosed stone chambers containing obstacles, **breakable walls**, and **floor traps** (spike pits, acid tiles, flame grates that deal flat hazard damage when entered or when an enemy is *Shoved* onto them).
   - **Breakable Walls:** Any player or enemy adjacent to a breakable wall can spend their **Main Action** to smash it down, opening new corridors and sightlines.
4. **Small / Cramped Dungeon Room (Limited Space — $6\times6$):**
   - **Layout:** A claustrophobic, compact $6\times6$ grid with tight corridors, traps, and breakable walls.
   - **Tactical Focus:** Limits kiting and movement space, forcing intense close-quarters brawls and making AoE blasts and *Shove* knockbacks into traps extremely lethal.
5. **Boss Room (Arena with Cover Pillars — $8\times8$):**
   - **Layout:** A focused $8\times8$ ritual/boss chamber featuring **Indestructible Cover Pillars**.
   - **Unblockable Boss Attacks & Pillar Cover:** Bosses can telegraph devastating **Unblockable Attacks** that ignore both Passive DEF and Active Defensive Stance. To survive, players must move behind a **Cover Pillar** (breaking line of sight from the Boss) before the attack resolves!

### Obstacle Traversal Formula (Question 129)
The movement cost to traverse an obstacle is:
$$\text{Total Movement Cost} = 1 \text{ (base tile entry)} + \max(0, 2H - 2J)$$
Where $H$ is the obstacle height and $J$ is the character's Jump stat.
- If a character's Jump is equal to or greater than the obstacle height ($J \ge H$), the vertical obstacle penalty is **0**, so vaulting across costs just 1 normal movement tile!
- *Example:* A height-2 fence ($H=2$) with Jump 1 ($J=1$) costs $1 + (4 - 2) = 3$ movement spaces. With Jump 2 ($J=2$), it costs just $1 + 0 = 1$ movement space.

### Moving Through and Jumping Over Units (Question 131)
- **Allied Units:** Characters can always move through allied units freely at **0 additional cost** (costing standard 1 tile per ally space). Characters cannot end their turn on an ally's tile.
- **Basic Enemies (Height 2):** Characters **CANNOT** simply walk through enemy spaces. However, a character can **jump/vault over a basic enemy** if and only if:
  $$\text{Jump} > \text{Enemy Height}$$
  Because basic enemies have Height 2, a character must have **Jump $\ge 3$** to vault over an enemy! If Jump is $\le 2$, the enemy completely blocks movement through that tile.
  - The landing tile immediately past the enemy must be unoccupied.
  - Crossing the enemy tile costs $1 + \max(0, 2(2) - 2J) = 1 + 0 = 1$ tile (since $J \ge 3 > 2$).
- **Bosses:** Bosses are massive and possess dynamic defensive presence. **Bosses cannot be jumped over** under any circumstances without flight or teleportation abilities.

## 11. Moves and abilities (Confirmed - Questions 133, 135, 136)

### Equipped Moves
- Characters can learn an unlimited number of moves throughout their adventures.
- Characters can equip a **maximum of 4 moves** at any given time (changed only outside battle).
- Basic attacks are universal, available to all characters, and **do not consume a move slot**.

### The Energy System (Question 133)
- Characters have a maximum **Energy pool of 10 Energy** (represented on the sheet/website as 10 discrete pips or a gauge).
- Players can cosmetically re-flavor Energy to suit their character (e.g. Goku's *Ki*, SpongeBob's *Bubbles/Silliness*, a wizard's *Mana*, a fighter's *Stamina*, a robot's *Battery*).
- **Energy Generation:**
  - Basic attacks cost **0 Energy** to perform.
  - Every successful basic attack hit generates **+2 Energy** (up to the cap of 10).
- **Starting Energy:** Characters begin each combat encounter with **0 Energy** (or starting energy granted by special passives/items), building up their pool as combat heats up.

### No Cooldowns & Move Freedom (Question 135)
- Special moves have **NO artificial turn cooldowns**.
- Moves cost varying amounts of Energy (e.g., Minor abilities cost 2–3 Energy; Signature attacks cost 4–6 Energy; Ultimate Finishers cost 8–10 Energy).
- A character can repeat, chain, or alternate their moves as often as their Energy and turn actions permit. If a player saves up 10 Energy and has actions/extra turns, they can unleash back-to-back special abilities freely!

### Magic Attack Damage Formula (Question 136)
Abilities are categorized into physical, magic, or utility:
- **Physical Moves:** Deal damage scaling with **ATK**, reduced flatly by target's **P.DEF** (minimum 1).
- **Magic Moves:** Deal damage scaling with **Intelligence**, reduced flatly by target's **M.DEF** (minimum 1):
  $$\text{Magic Damage} = \text{Base Move Damage} + \text{Intelligence}$$
  $$\text{Net Magic Damage Received} = \max(1, \text{Magic Damage} - \text{Target M.DEF})$$
- **True / Piercing Moves:** Rare abilities explicitly bypassing P.DEF and/or M.DEF.

### Move Range, Targeting & Area of Effect (AoE) (Confirmed)
When players or the DM design custom moves, each move specifies:
1. **Range & Range Bands:**
   - **Range 1 (Melee / Touch):** Requires adjacency to the target.
   - **Range $1\text{–}X$ (Standard Ranged):** Can target any tile from adjacent out to $X$ tiles away.
   - **Range $X\text{–}Y$ (Range Band / Dead Zone):** For example, `5-9` blocks away. Target must be within this band (units closer than 5 cannot be targeted).
2. **Targeting Type:**
   - **Single-Target:** Directly targets a single enemy or ally unit.
   - **Area of Effect (AoE):** Targets an area or point on the grid, affecting multiple tiles and units.
3. **AoE Dimensions & Impact:**
   - **Defined Shape/Size:** e.g., `3x3` box (centered on a targeted tile, affecting the center tile plus all 8 surrounding adjacent tiles), `1x4` line, or `Diamond` spread.
   - **Primary Focus:** The center tile is the main target, while surrounding tiles take splash damage or effects (or uniform AoE damage as specified by the ability).

## 12. Passive abilities (Confirmed - Question 134)

### Active Passive Slots
- Characters have **4 Active Passive Slots** (providing symmetrical balance with the 4 equipped move slots).
- Characters can learn an unlimited number of passives across their journey, but may only equip up to 4 active passives at once.
- Active passives can only be swapped outside of combat.

### Innate Passives
- Innate passives represent the core identity of a character (e.g. Goku's *Saiyan Resilience*, SpongeBob's *Spongy Body*).
- **Innate passives occupy active passive slots** (typically 1 or 2 slots), ensuring that characters with powerful intrinsic perks naturally have fewer open slots for generic learned passives.
- Innate passives are permanent unless altered by rare multiverse artifacts or pivotal story events.

### Learned Passives
- Discovered and unlocked via level progression, quests, exploration, trainer NPCs, or boss rewards.
- Learned passives can be swapped freely between battles into any remaining active passive slots.

## 14. Equipment, inventory, and currency (Confirmed - Question 137)

### Equipment Slots
Every character possesses **4 Fixed Equipment Slots**:
1. **Weapon:** Sets base attack damage or range, provides ATK or INT bonuses, and can alter damage type (Physical/Magic/Piercing).
2. **Armour:** Grants flat **P.DEF** and/or **M.DEF** bonuses, bonus HP, or hazard resistances.
3. **Accessory:** Enhances utility stats like **Move**, **Jump**, **Accuracy**, **Speed**, or **Luck**.
4. **Multiverse Relic / Special Item:** Grants unique passive perks, TT bonuses (e.g. +50% TT chronometers), active consumable slots, or unique multiverse interaction abilities.

### Inventory & Trading
- **Inventory capacity is unlimited**.
- Items can be traded freely between party members outside of battle.
- Equipment is not automatically character-bound, though specific gear may have stat prerequisites (e.g. Minimum 10 ATK to wield a colossal blade).

### Multiverse Economy
- **One universal currency** across all realms and dimensions.
- Basic consumables and standard equipment are sold reliably in multiverse shops.
- Selected weapons and armor can be upgraded at blacksmiths or planar enchanters using currency and rare upgrade materials.

## 15. Health, defeat, and recovery (Confirmed - Question 139)

### Survival Recovery Model (Strict Survival)
Health is a scarce, precious resource across the multiverse:
- **Zero Automatic Post-Battle Healing:** Characters do **NOT** automatically regenerate HP after a battle. Damage sustained in combat persists until actively treated.
- **Active Healing:** Restoring HP between battles requires:
  - Consumable items (e.g. Senzu beans, Krabby Patties, health potions, rations).
  - Healing abilities and spells (consuming character Energy).
- **Long Rest:** Taking a prolonged rest (camping in a fortified safe area or staying at an inn) completely restores all characters to **100% maximum HP**.
- **Defeat & Knockout:**
  - In standard encounters, reaching 0 HP knocks the character unconscious until revived by an ally, an item, or when combat concludes (reviving at 1 HP).
  - In boss or planar crisis encounters, reaching 0 HP can risk lasting narrative injuries or true defeat as determined by the stakes of the battle.

## 16. Party structure & Combat Scaling (Confirmed - Question 140)

- **Flexible Party Size:** There is no arbitrary hard cap on active combat party members. All active players in the session participate directly on the battle grid.
- **Dynamic Encounter Scaling:** The DM and the website's encounter engine scale the quantity, HP, and stats of enemies to fit the size and level of the player party.
- **Manual Player Control:** Each player controls their own character, managing movement, actions, active blocks, and energy expenditure.
- **Teamwork Perks:** Coordination is rewarded through the tactical menu (e.g., using *Shove* to push an enemy into an ally's range, or *Assist* to grant an ally +2 on their attack roll).

## 20. The DND2 Website & Multiplayer Architecture (Confirmed)

The DND2 web platform functions as a real-time **Smart Tabletop Assistant** designed for voice-call multiplayer sessions.

### 1. Multiplayer Lobby & Role-Based Permissions
- **Host & Join:**
  - The DM hosts a game lobby (generating a room code / invite link).
  - Players enter the room code to join the session.
- **Strict Role Permissions:**
  - **Player Permissions:** Each player is assigned to their own character. Players can only move their own token, spend their own Energy, choose their own moves/actions, and roll their own active blocks and d20 checks.
  - **DM Omnipotent Control:** The DM controls all enemy tokens, configures the environment, spawns breakable walls, sets difficulty/loot, and has 1-click manual override privileges over any stat, HP value, or dice result.

### 2. Character & Enemy Portrait Uploads
- **Custom Image Uploads:**
  - Players can upload custom image files (or paste image URLs) for their character portraits.
  - The uploaded portrait is displayed on the character sheet and rendered inside their circular token on the tactical grid for all connected players to see in real time.
  - The DM can similarly upload custom portraits for any enemy, NPC, or boss created.

### 3. DM Enemy Creator & Encounter Launcher
- **Dynamic Enemy Builder:**
  - The DM can rapidly build or template enemies by specifying: Name, Portrait, Range (e.g. 1, 1–4, 5–9), Stats (HP, ATK, P.DEF, M.DEF, Move, Jump, INT, LUK, ACC, SPD), equipped moves, and passives.
- **Combat Start Trigger:**
  - Once combatants are placed, the DM clicks "Start Combat". The website initializes the Accumulating Action Gauge, populates the dynamic turn timeline, and begins the battle.

### 4. Battlefield Engine: Illustrated Battlemaps, Click-to-Move & Breakable Walls
- **Illustrated Battlemap Backgrounds:**
  - Instead of plain solid-colored tiles, the battlefield grid renders over **top-down illustrated battlemap artwork** (e.g., torch-lit cobblestone dungeon interiors, overgrown forest ruins, volcanic caverns) with a crisp, semi-transparent tactical grid overlay.
- **Click-to-Move Selection & Highlighting:**
  - Clicking on your character token activates movement mode and **highlights all valid reachable grid spaces** in a glowing movement diamond.
  - The pathfinding automatically accounts for walls (impassable until broken), enemy collision (vaultable only if $\text{Jump} > \text{Height}$), difficult terrain (2 Move/tile), and elevated obstacles ($1 + \max(0, 2H - 2J)$).
  - Clicking any highlighted tile moves the character token to that space and deducts the movement cost.
- **Randomized Terrain Generation:**
  - The DM can generate randomized tactical maps with customizable themes:
    - **Interior:** Tight dungeon corridors, indoor chambers, chokepoints, and rooms.
    - **Exterior:** Open wilderness, ruins, scattered cliffs, and wide fields.
  - Random elevation heights ($H0, H1, H2, H3$) and terrain types (normal, difficult mud/water, hazards).
- **Interactive Breakable Walls:**
  - Breakable walls and obstacles are placed on the battlefield.
  - Any unit (player or enemy) adjacent to a breakable wall can spend their **Main Action** to demolish the wall, clearing the tile into rubble and opening new tactical lines of sight and movement corridors.

### 5. Range & AoE Visualizer
- **Range Band Checking:**
  - The grid visually highlights valid target tiles based on the attack's range profile:
    - **Range 1:** Adjacent tiles only.
    - **Range Bands (e.g. 5–9):** Highlights tiles between distance 5 and 9 from the attacker; tiles closer than 5 are in the "dead zone" and cannot be targeted.
- **Area of Effect (AoE) Templates:**
  - AoE moves (such as a `3x3` blast) show an interactive hover preview: the center target tile is highlighted as the primary impact point, with the surrounding 8 tiles illuminated for splash damage/effects.

### 6. Tech Stack
- Built using **React + TypeScript + Tailwind CSS** with real-time peer-to-peer / room state sync, coupled with local-first `localStorage` JSON backups.
